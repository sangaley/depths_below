A destroyer drawn the game's way, with a spinal gun built from parts in the hull and three turrets on the deck above it. How the numbers work

Gauge
60 mm, plus 55 mm per gauge increaser touching the gun, up to 8 (500 mm).
Shell
The sample round is an AP head, one solid body and two propellant casings: 3 × gauge long. It has to fit the autoloader, which takes 1 m of shell per cell of loader.
Barrel
The casings need 1 m of barrel per 100 mm of gauge to burn. Short of that, muzzle velocity falls with the square root of the shortfall. Longer barrels also tighten the spread.
Reload
Each loader takes 2.4 s × (gauge ÷ 100)1.35 × shell length, sped up 25% per ammo clip. Loaders work in parallel.
Heat
Every shot heats the gun by (gauge ÷ 100)1.5 × 0.6; coolers carry off 0.6 a second each. A hot gun waits.
Recoil
Recoil a shot doesn't sink into absorbers widens the spread. A muzzle brake halves recoil and costs a little accuracy.
Rails
Each rail magnet adds 90 m/s and draws 1.5 MW while charging.
Turret
Turn speed = 260 × traverse motors ÷ turret mass (t), between 2 and 80°/s. Everything on the turret deck counts toward that mass. Loaders kept below the ring don't, but every shell then rides up the hoist, at one per 1.5 s.
Top wins
Where a turret covers the hull, a round meets the turret first. The ring and hoist under it can only be reached once the turret block above them is gone.

These are first numbers to argue with, written so the game can use the same formulas. The shell itself is fixed here; designing it is Phase 4.
